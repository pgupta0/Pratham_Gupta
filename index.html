01 Himanshu Travel Line
Growth & Operations · 2023 to 2025

A fleet losing drivers faster than it could replace them

The situation

A transport operator with corporate contracts it could not grow. Drivers kept leaving, so runs went late, so clients complained. Pricing had not been revisited in years, and nobody was measuring what fuel, repairs and paperwork were actually costing.

What I did

Took the commercial side end to end. Rebuilt corporate pricing, negotiated two new accounts myself and expanded five existing fleets. Raised financing against the signed contracts so we could actually add vehicles, then built a premium tier on the upgraded fleet. After that I went at the cost base line by line, and fixed retention, because every complaint traced back to a driver who had just quit.

What changed ₹2.5Cr

closed in contracts, with ₹4Cr financed behind them. Driver turnover fell 63% and late deliveries 47%. I took ₹18.2L a year out of the cost base while doing it.

02 BigBasket
HO4, T2 Operations · summer 2026

Twenty-two cities were losing stock and nobody could say why

The situation

Tier-2 cities reported write-off once a month, so by the time a number moved the loss was already months old. I was responsible for all twenty-two of them from a spreadsheet, for cities I had never set foot in.

What I did

Went city by city, two or three calls a week with the worst ten, asking questions at SKU level until the causes came apart. Built the daily reporting the cities now run on. Then I flew down and walked the floor myself, a dark store and a distribution centre, including one visit nobody was expecting.

What changed 12

root causes traced. The sharpest was a loop nobody had spotted: writing stock off without correcting the minimum quantity silently re-triggered the same purchase order, so cities kept losing the same item. Cluster write-off went 0.99% to 0.93% of sales, and cities holding target went 7 to 15.

"Before this, managing Tier-2 cities from head office felt like casting darts into a blind space."

From my own field report, after the first store visit
03 IKONA
Influencer & Brand · 2024 to 2025

A client wanted creator reach and had no creators

The situation

No roster, no briefing process, and no way to tell whether any of it was working. The whole function existed as an intention and nothing else.

What I did

Sourced and vetted creators one at a time until there were fifty. Built a campus ambassador layer across eight colleges to reach students directly rather than through a feed. Then wrote a standard brief, because output was swinging wildly depending on who happened to write it.

What changed +40%

client social reach, 50+ creators onboarded from zero, and over 200 direct student interactions through the ambassador network.
